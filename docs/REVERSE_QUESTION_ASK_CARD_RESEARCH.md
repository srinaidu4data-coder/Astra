## 1. Executive summary

### 1.1 What is established about employment interviews generally

1. Employment interviews can show **criterion-related validity** for job performance, moderated by interview **content**, **structure**, and **criterion type**. **[P]** McDaniel, Whetzel, Schmidt, & Maurer (1994), *Journal of Applied Psychology* — 245 coefficients, *N* ≈ 86,311.  
2. **Structure is a major moderator** of interview validity: more structured interviews tend to show higher validity than unstructured ones. **[P]** Huffcutt & Arthur (1994), *JAP*; **[P]** Campion, Palmer, & Campion (1997) structure components; **[S]** Levashina et al. (2014) narrative/quantitative review.  
3. The bulk of selection-interview science concerns **questions asked by interviewers** and **how applicants answer them**, not questions applicants ask interviewers. **[S]** Macan (2009) review; **[P]** Hartwell et al. (2019) on interviewer question types predicting performance.

**Implication:** Any reverse-question product must be sized as a **peripheral signal** relative to answer quality under structured selection. **[D]** from [P]/[S] above.

### 1.2 What is established about *applicant-initiated* reverse questions

Primary literature is **sparse**. The most detailed experimental evidence located and fully extracted in this investigation:

**Heimbaugh, H. G. (2016).** *Questioning the Questioner: The Effect of Applicant Questions on Interview Outcomes.* PhD dissertation, University of Missouri–St. Louis (advisor: Therese Macan). Full PDF reviewed (176 pp.). **[P]**

| Finding (Heimbaugh) | Evidence detail | Certainty for *that study* |
|---------------------|-----------------|----------------------------|
| Asking vs not asking can change **attributions** (e.g., job interest, sometimes P–O fit, personality impressions) | Study 1–2 planned comparisons; interest effects sometimes large (e.g., Study 2 novel Q vs none: *d* ≈ 1.40) | **Moderate** (lab) |
| **No significant main effect** of applicant questions on **hiring ratings** | Study 1: *F*(2,232)=2.59, *p*=.08, η²=.02; Study 2: *F*(2,225)=0.38, *p*=.69, η²<.01 | **Moderate–high** *within this design* |
| **Interview performance** affected hiring ratings when manipulated | Study 2: well vs marginal performance, η²=.15 | **High** *within this design* |
| Novel (not website-answerable) questions rated **higher quality** than website-answerable | Study 1 *d*≈.43; Study 2 *d*≈.56 | **Moderate** |
| Novelty did **not** consistently outperform “any questions” on most attributions | Author discussion: ask-vs-not effects often medium–large; novelty gaps smaller | **Moderate** |
| Interviewer **expectation** that applicants should ask moderated **P–O fit** | When expect questions: askers > non-askers on fit (e.g., Study 2 novel vs none *d*≈.64) | **Moderate** |
| Author conclusion: performance has **larger impact** than questions/résumé under strong answers | Overall Discussion | **Author interpretation [P]** |

**Critical limits of this pillar [P]:** employed student raters (most do not regularly hire); single job (management trainee); video between-subjects design; author notes design may **underestimate** contrast effects vs multi-candidate slates.

Related primary / near-primary:

- **Taylor, Friedman, & Coolsen (2013)** — applicant question content and impressions (extraversion/openness); relatively small rating effects for well-qualified applicants as summarized in Heimbaugh. Full independent re-read of this paper was **not** completed. **[P/S mixed — partial]**  
- **Tullar (1989)** — relational control patterns in recruiting interviews (*JAP*). Successful candidates often **complement** interviewer dominance (more submissive when interviewer dominant). **[P]** abstract/citation-level use; full re-analysis not performed.  
- **No multi-employer field RCT** of reverse-question training → offer rates was found. **[U]**

### 1.3 Bottom line (evidence-proportionate)

| Claim | Status |
|-------|--------|
| Strong answers matter more than reverse questions for hire ratings *in the best available experiment* | **Supported [P]** Heimbaugh |
| Reverse questions can raise **interest / some fit / personality** impressions | **Supported [P]** Heimbaugh (lab) |
| Reverse questions **reliably raise hire ratings or offer rates** | **Not established** — Heimbaugh null main effects; no field RCT **[U / fails]** |
| One “mind-bending” question “cracks” the interview | **Not established** — contradicted by answer-dominance pattern **[fails]** |
| An “Ask” card is a reasonable **optional signal aid** if gated and honest | **Design judgment [D]** consistent with [P] interest findings + [Pr] norms; **not** proven EV-positive |

## 3. Detailed findings by topic

### 3.1 Selection-interview validity (context)

**Material claims**

1. Interview validity depends on content (situational / job-related / psychological), structure (structured vs unstructured; board vs individual), and criterion. **[P]** McDaniel et al. (1994).  
2. Structure is a major moderator of validity for entry-level jobs. **[P]** Huffcutt & Arthur (1994).  
3. Structure can be defined as enhancements that increase standardization of questions and evaluation. **[P]** Campion et al. (1997); **[S]** Levashina et al. (2014).

**Uncertainty:** These metas do not measure applicant reverse questions. Using them only sizes reverse questions as **outside the main validity engine**. **[D]**

### 3.2 Applicant reverse questions — experimental evidence

**Material claims (Heimbaugh 2016 only, unless noted)**

1. End-of-interview applicant questions were experimentally manipulated after interviewer closed with a standard prompt. **[P]** Methods.  
2. **Job interest:** askers rated higher than non-askers; in Study 2, novel > website-answerable > none on interest (all pairwise comparisons significant as reported). **[P]**  
3. **P–O fit:** higher for askers when raters *expect* applicants to ask; weaker/null when raters are merely “open” without expectation. **[P]**  
4. **Extraversion / openness:** generally higher for askers than non-askers (pattern differs slightly Study 1 vs 2). **[P]**  
5. **Motivation / preparation / conscientiousness:** more differences in Study 2 (when interview performance varied) than Study 1 (uniform strong performance). **[P]**  
6. **Hiring ratings:** no significant main effect of question condition; significant main effect of interview performance (Study 2). **[P]**  
7. Attributions of P–O fit, motivation, and conscientiousness predicted hiring ratings in regression (Study 2, *R*²=.79 for the attribution set as reported). **[P]**  
8. Author: interview performance had larger impact than questions. **[P]** discussion.

**Uncertainty / adversarial caveats**

- Null hire main effect ≠ proof of zero total effect under other designs (within-subjects, real HMs, stronger question content). **[U]**  
- Interest ratings may partly reflect script completion (“they prolonged the interview”). **[T/speculation]**  
- Student raters limit external validity. **[P]** author limitation.

### 3.3 Related primary interaction evidence

1. Relational control: successful applicants in one exploratory study tended toward **complementary** patterns (submissive when interviewer dominant). **[P]** Tullar (1989).  
2. **Uncertainty:** Does not directly test reverse-question content; implies aggressive “peer challenge” may violate observed successful patterns in that sample. **[T]**

## 4. Major conclusions (final, adversarial-hardened)

Each conclusion states: **claim · evidence · what would disprove it · residual confidence**.

### C1 — Interview answer performance is a stronger driver of hire ratings than applicant reverse questions *in the best available experiment*

- **Evidence:** Heimbaugh Study 2 performance η²=.15; question main effects on hire ns. **[P]**  
- **Disproof path:** Within-subjects multi-candidate field study with large question effect on offers.  
- **Confidence:** **High** *for that paradigm*; **moderate** as universal law (adversarial review demoted universality).

### C2 — Applicant reverse questions can increase perceived job interest (and sometimes fit/personality impressions)

- **Evidence:** Heimbaugh interest and conditional fit/personality results. **[P]**  
- **Disproof path:** Replications with real HMs showing null interest effects.  
- **Confidence:** **Moderate** (lab, single stimulus family).

### C3 — Applicant reverse questions are not established as a reliable direct lever on hiring ratings or offers

- **Evidence:** Heimbaugh null hire main effects **[P]**; absence of field RCTs **[U]**.  
- **Disproof path:** High-powered field trial with significant offer effects.  
- **Confidence:** **Moderate–high** that “reliable direct lever” is **not currently evidenced**.

### C4 — “Gate Ask on strong answers” is a design policy, not an empirical necessity

- **Evidence for gating:** Desire to avoid weak answer + flashy Q theater **[D]**; answer dominance **[P]**.  
- **Evidence against gating as law:** Heimbaugh found *more* attribution effects of questions when performance varied (Study 2) — weak performers might still gain interest impressions. **[P]**  
- **Confidence that gating is mandatory:** **Low**.  
- **Confidence that answer quality still matters more for hire ratings:** **High** in Heimbaugh.

### C5 — Preferring end-of-interview placement is a low-risk default, not a proven optimum

- **Evidence for end as studied/normative:** Heimbaugh **[P]**; career norms **[Pr]**.  
- **Evidence for end > mid:** **None comparative [U]**.  
- **Mid required in some formats:** Consulting case clarifying **[S]**.  
- **Confidence for “end only”:** **Low**. **Confidence for “end is safe default in general HM interviews”:** **Moderate [D]**.

### C6 — Prefer job-related questions; treat pure website-FAQ questions as lower quality

- **Evidence:** Quality ratings favor novel **[P]**; practice against Googleable FAQs **[Pr]**.  
- **Limit:** Novelty does not always win on attributions **[P]**.  
- **Confidence:** **Moderate** for quality; **low–moderate** for hire impact.

### C7 — Collaborative tone is a risk-management default; prosecutorial superiority is unproven

- **Evidence for risk:** SCM **[P]**; gender-assertiveness backlash literature **[P]**; Tullar complementarity **[P]**.  
- **Evidence for hire benefit of collaborative reverse Qs:** **Not direct [U]**.  
- **Confidence for default collaborative tone:** **Moderate [D]**; for “always better for offers”: **Low**.

### C8 — In-process Ask and post-offer reverse interview should be treated as different interventions

- **Evidence:** Different goals and timing in practitioner accounts **[S]**; no study equating them.  
- **Confidence:** **High** as conceptual hygiene; **N/A** as empirics.

### C9 — An Ask feature may be justified as an optional signal/information aid, not as an offer-rate product claim

- **Evidence for optional aid:** Interest/fit attribution movement **[P]**; engagement norms **[Pr]**.  
- **Evidence against “must ship / greatest card”:** Null hire main effects **[P]**; cognitive load / authenticity risks **[U/D]**; opportunity cost vs answer quality **[D]** from validity literature **[P]**.  
- **Confidence for “may ship as optional aid with honest claims”:** **Moderate [D]**.  
- **Confidence for “greatest card / cracks interviews”:** **Very low — not supported**.

### C10 — A single mind-bending question does not generally “crack” the interview

- **Evidence:** Answer dominance pattern **[P]**; no RCT of cracking questions; peak–end/curiosity only **[T]**.  
- **Confidence:** **High** that the general “cracking” claim is unsupported.

## 5. Product implications (explicitly design-tier)

All items below are **[D]** unless noted. They are **consistent with** evidence but **not mandated** by it.

### 5.1 Recommended product posture

1. Optimize **answer quality** first (structured Hook/Proof/Close). **[D]** from **[P]** validity + Heimbaugh performance.  
2. Offer **Ask** as optional, sparse, end-biased reverse-question assistance. **[D]**  
3. **Never market** offer-rate lift or “cracks the interview.” **[D]** from C3/C10.  
4. Prefer **short, job-related, non-FAQ, collaborative** questions. **[D]** from **[P]** quality + **[Pr]** + risk **[T]**.  
5. Separate **post-offer diligence** content from in-process Ask. **[D]** from **[S]**.  
6. Respect **domain isolation** (only Role/Job/conversation) — product integrity issue from prior Astra work, not reverse-Q RCTs. **[D]** product history.  
7. For consulting-style sessions, allow **mid clarifying** mode distinct from “impressive end Ask.” **[D]** from **[S]**.  
8. Async video: Ask may be **prep / follow-up email** only. **[D]** from **[S]** async formats.

### 5.2 Example card contract (non-empirical)

```
ASK  (optional)
When: after your answer is complete / when they invite questions
[One short question]

Not a guarantee. Prefer real job constraints over website FAQs.
```

### 5.3 Explicit non-features of “evidence-based”

- SPIN depth, peak–end timing, dopamine/curiosity, Shannon utility scores — **internal heuristics [T/D] only**.  
- Rate caps (e.g., 1–2 per interview) — **[D]** from isolation/choice-overload **[T]**, not reverse-Q dose trials.  
- Replacing Cool with Ask — **[D]** preference; no head-to-head study.

## 10. One-page verdict

**Science says:**  
In the strongest primary package we fully extracted, **how you answer interviewer questions moves hire ratings; whether you ask reverse questions mainly moves impressions such as interest (and sometimes fit), not hire ratings as a main effect.** **[P]**

**Practice says:**  
Candidates are often expected to ask *something* thoughtful at the end; FAQ and early compensation questions are discouraged. **[Pr]**

**Transfer science says:**  
Tone, timing, curiosity, and memory *might* matter — untested as reverse-question treatments in hiring. **[T]**

**Product should say:**  
Optional Ask assistance is defensible as a **signal and preparation aid** if claims stay modest, answers stay primary, and domain/timing/tone risks are controlled. **[D]**

**Product must not say:**  
That one reverse question makes the candidate “the one,” cracks the interview, or is backed by offer-rate science.

## Appendix A — Cross-field lenses (transfer only; not hire RCTs)

Material claims below are **[T]** unless noted. They inform **design constraints**, not offer-rate guarantees. See main report C9/C10.

### Neuroscience & anatomy
- **Working memory / prefrontal load:** Capacity limits (~3–5 chunks) motivate one reverse question, not a menu (Cowan tradition). **[T]**
- **Curiosity–dopamine–hippocampus:** Gruber et al. 2014 — curiosity can enhance encoding; **not** tested on interview reverse questions. **[P domain / T to hiring]**
- **Turn-taking timing:** Sub-second gaps (Stivers et al. 2009) → never interrupt mid-turn. **[P CA / T to product timing]**
- **Fovea vs periphery (visual):** Speak UI already dims periphery; Ask rail uses high-contrast cyan for isolation without theory chrome. **[D]**

### Biological / evolutionary social cognition
- **Warmth × competence** (Fiske SCM) → collaborative Ask tone. **[P social / T]**
- **Status / expectation states** → reverse Q is a specific competence cue only after earned floor. **[T]**

### Defense / adversarial systems
- **Fail-closed gates:** rate limit, risk filters, streaming suppress = defense-in-depth (like access control). **[D]**
- **Honest IM only:** no invented company intel (deceptive IM literature). **[P IM / D]**
- **Red-team tone:** prosecutorial lexicon blocked. **[D]**

### Calculus / information theory (formal design, not fitted models)
- Utility sketch: \(U(q) \approx I(\text{job state}; a_q)\cdot V - \lambda R(q)\) — maximize information about valued constraints subject to recklessness penalty. **[D]** not estimated from data.
- **Rate limit** as discrete budget constraint (sparse sampling). **[D]**

### Astrophysics (metaphor only)
- Sparse high-mass Ask event bends conversation “geodesics”; continuous thrusters (spam Qs) are noise. **Internal metaphor only — never UI.** **[D]**

### Political science
- **Agenda control / question privileges** (who defines the next topic) maps to turn allocation. **[T]**
- **Legitimacy of challenge** depends on institutional role (junior vs principal). Soften first-card Ask. **[D]**

### AI / ML systems engineering
- **No online model required** for v1 craft (deterministic templates + anchors) → latency, privacy, eval stability. **[D]**
- **Eval harness before ship** (fixtures) — Karpathy: measure what you ship.
- **Graph-of-gates** for observability (trace path in planAskRail).
- **Homogenization risk** of AI Qs: templates + domain anchors reduce pure generic sludge; authenticity still **[U]**.

## Appendix B — Multi-lab design review (simulated bar)

### Karpathy bar
- Pure functions, offline eval, no training loop cosplay.
- Ship smallest thing that can be measured (show/hide + question text).
- Prefer boring templates over “mind-bending” generators.

### OpenAI product bar
- Progressive disclosure; no formula chrome; user always can ignore Ask.
- Fail soft when gates fail (Cool may still show).

### Anthropic bar
- Honest claims: no “cracks the interview.”
- Safety: block comp-early, FAQ bait, prosecutorial tone.
- Prefer collaborative framing (warmth + competence).

### Microsoft bar
- Accessibility: reduced-motion CSS; short speakable line.
- Enterprise: domain isolation from Role/Job only; no skill pooling.

**Consensus decision:** Ship Ask as **gated end rail superseding Cool**, with harness + eval, marketing limited to “optional reverse question when ready.”
